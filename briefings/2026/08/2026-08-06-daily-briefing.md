**Source:** MarketWatch

**Summary (EN):**
The S&P 500's recent five-day rally appears to be driven more by mechanical options market dynamics and options positioning than by fundamental macroeconomic improvements. High volumes of aggressive options trading, particularly around zero-days-to-expiration (0DTE) contracts, forced market makers to buy underlying equities to hedge their exposure, fueling an upward feedback loop. This mechanical buying, known as a gamma squeeze, artificially inflates stock prices and heightens market vulnerability to sudden reversals. Investors are warned that such rallies lack a solid valuation foundation, suggesting heightened near-term volatility. The trend underscores how derivatives trading increasingly dictates index movement rather than traditional corporate earnings or economic indicators.
